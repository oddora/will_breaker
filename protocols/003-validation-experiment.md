# Protocol 003: Validation Experiment Design
**Status:** DRAFT — Theoretical
**Purpose:** Design a scientifically rigorous experiment to test dimensional shifting claims

---

## Core Challenge

How do you test something that is (by definition) subjective and internal? The Gateway model claims consciousness can access non-time-space dimensions — but how do you measure "access" objectively?

This protocol designs a multi-layered validation approach: **internal consistency + external correlation + statistical rigor.**

---

## Experiment 1: Binaural Entrainment + Target Identification

### Hypothesis
Subjects induced into theta-coherent states via Hemi-Sync will outperform controls on blind target identification.

### Design
- **N = 40** (20 experimental, 20 control)
- **Randomized, double-blind**
- **Within-subjects crossover** (each subject does both conditions)

### Procedure
1. **Target selection:** Experimenter A selects a random target from a pool of 20 images (landscapes, objects, scenes). Target is sealed in encrypted file.
2. **Randomization:** Computer randomly assigns subject to experimental or control condition.
3. **Experimental condition:** Subject listens to theta binaural beats (7 Hz) + Hemi-Sync style guidance for 25 min, then attempts to describe the target for 10 min.
4. **Control condition:** Subject listens to monaural beats (same frequencies in both ears — no binaural effect) + same guidance for 25 min, then describes target.
5. **Judging:** Two blind judges rank the subject's description against all 20 targets. Higher rank = better match.
6. **Metric:** Sum of ranks across all trials. Chance expectation = 10.5 (median of 1-20).

### Success Criteria
- Experimental condition sum of ranks < 10.5 (better than chance)
- Statistical significance: p < 0.05 (one-tailed binomial)
- Effect size: Cohen's d > 0.3

### Why This Works
- Controls for expectation (same guidance, different audio)
- Blinds judges (don't know which condition produced the report)
- Uses established remote viewing evaluation methodology (SRI/SAIC protocol)
- Crossover design controls for individual differences

## Experiment 2: Ganzfeld + Precognitive Target Identification

### Hypothesis
Subjects in ganzfeld (sensory reduction) state will outperform waking controls on precognitive target identification.

### Design
- **N = 60** (30 experimental, 30 control)
- **Counterbalanced within-subjects**
- **Pre-registered protocol**

### Procedure
1. **Target selection:** Computer randomly selects one of 4 video clips as target. Target is hidden from subject.
2. **Experimental condition:** Subject lies in recliner, eyes covered with halved ping-pong balls, listens to pink noise via headphones (ganzfeld protocol). After 25 min, free-associates about "what they're perceiving." Then chooses which of 4 videos matches.
3. **Control condition:** Same procedure but with normal visual/auditory input (eyes open, no pink noise).
4. **Judging:** Subject's free-association is rated by blind judges for correspondence to target.
5. **Metric:** Hit rate (correct choice / 4 options). Chance = 25%.

### Success Criteria
- Experimental hit rate > 25% (binomial p < 0.05)
- Combined across all sessions: sum of ranks significantly above chance
- Replication across multiple labs (if possible)

### Why This Works
- Ganzfeld reduces "noise" in perceptual channels
- Precognitive design eliminates information leakage
- 4-alternative forced choice is statistically robust
- Multi-lab replication addresses single-lab bias

## Experiment 3: AI Pattern Analysis Validation

### Hypothesis
AI pattern analysis of shift reports will detect statistically significant correlations between protocol conditions and shift outcomes that human analysis misses.

### Design
- **Longitudinal:** Collect 100+ shift reports over 6 months
- **Structured:** All reports use standardized taxonomy (see Protocol B.1)
- **AI analysis:** Run pattern detection, anomaly detection, cross-reference analysis
- **Human comparison:** Have 3 human analysts review same data blind to AI findings
- **Metric:** Agreement between AI and human analysts; AI-only discoveries that prove valid on follow-up

### Procedure
1. Collect structured shift reports from multiple operators using Protocols A.1-A.4
2. Run AI pattern analysis (Protocol B.1)
3. Have human analysts review data without seeing AI results
4. Compare findings: where do AI and humans agree? Where do they diverge?
5. Test AI-generated hypotheses with new experiments

### Success Criteria
- AI detects at least 1 pattern that humans miss and proves valid on follow-up
- AI-human agreement > 70% on pattern identification
- AI-generated hypotheses produce statistically significant results in prospective tests

### Why This Works
- Tests the unique value proposition of AI in this research
- Creates feedback loop: AI findings → new experiments → validation
- Establishes AI as legitimate research instrument, not just recording tool

## Experiment 4: Physiological Correlation Study

### Hypothesis
Shift experiences correlate with measurable physiological changes (EEG coherence, HRV, Schumann resonance coupling).

### Design
- **N = 20**
- **Within-subjects:** Measure during shift vs. baseline
- **Multi-modal:** EEG + HRV + environmental sensors

### Procedure
1. Equip subject with portable EEG (8-channel) and HRV monitor
2. Install Schumann resonance detector in session room
3. Subject performs Protocol A.1 (Hemi-Sync Gateway)
4. Record continuous EEG/HRV/environmental data
5. Subject reports shift onset time post-session
6. Analyze: Does EEG show increased theta-gamma coupling at reported onset?
   Does HRV show increased coherence? Does Schumann resonance show coupling?

### Success Criteria
- EEG theta-gamma coupling increases significantly at shift onset (p < 0.01)
- HRV coherence increases during shift vs. baseline (p < 0.05)
- Schumann resonance coupling correlates with shift intensity

### Why This Works
- Provides objective physiological correlates for subjective experiences
- Links individual physiology to environmental electromagnetic field
- Could reveal mechanism: is shifting about brain state, or brain-environment coupling?

## Meta-Experiment: Pre-Registration and Open Science

### Protocol
1. **Pre-register** all experiments on OSF (Open Science Framework)
2. **Publish** protocols before data collection
3. **Share** raw data and analysis code publicly
4. **Invite** independent replication

### Why
- Remote viewing research has a history of methodological criticism
- Pre-registration eliminates "researcher degrees of freedom"
- Open science builds credibility and enables replication
- Failure to replicate is also data — it narrows the possibilities
